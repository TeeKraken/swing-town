# Swing Town

A classroom toy for the gerrymandering puzzle: draw districts in a made-up town, then slide a small, even swing in the vote and watch a greedy map lose almost every seat while a safer map holds.

**Use it:** https://swing-town.vercel.app (free, no sign-up).

## What it does

- **A made-up town.** 36 blocks in a 6 by 6 grid, 100 voters in each. The number on a block is how many vote orange; the rest vote purple. Orange and purple are just colors, never parties, and the town isn't a real place.
- **Three ready-made maps** of the same town, each with 6 districts of 6 blocks:
  - *Fair boxes:* plain boxes. Orange has 46.8% of the votes and wins 3 of 6 seats.
  - *Greedy (packed and cracked):* purple voters packed into one district, the rest cracked so orange wins 5 of 6: four thin wins, each less than four points above half, and one safer win.
  - *Safe seats:* orange's voters kept together, so orange wins only 3 seats, each at least ten points above half.
- **Draw your own map** by picking a district and tapping or dragging across blocks (or with the keyboard). It checks that every block is in a district, every district has exactly 6 blocks, and each district joins up side to side, and says in plain words what to fix.
- **A swing slider** from 10 points toward purple to 10 points toward orange. Every block and district is re-scored live, with seats per color and a chart (and table) of orange's seats at every swing for the current map.
- **A five-step story** for a class: meet the town, the greedy map, a 3-point swing that takes the greedy map from 5 seats to 1, the safe map that holds even at 10 points, and a challenge: draw a map that keeps a majority for orange (4 of 6) even after a 5-point swing toward purple. None of the ready-made maps meets it; a hint is there for anyone stuck.
- **Print this map:** a one-page printout (checked on A4 and US Letter), with the made-up-town and JRMF framing at the top, of the current map (numbers before any swing, district numbers, thick borders), each district's result, and a table of seats at every swing, to use alongside the paper puzzle.

Works from 375px wide with touch, mouse or keyboard, follows your system's light or dark mode, has visible keyboard focus, and respects "reduce motion". Nothing you draw is uploaded or saved. No account, no cookies, no analytics scripts, no third-party scripts, and no tracking by the app. Like any website's host, Vercel keeps request logs that can include IP addresses and referring sites. We only look at weekly totals of requests to load the app, grouped by referring site, to see whether people find it. We don't look at individual requests.

An unofficial companion to the [gerrymandering puzzle](https://jrmf.org/puzzle/gerrymandering) from the Julia Robinson Mathematics Festival. Not made by or affiliated with JRMF; none of their text or graphics is used.

## The math

All of it is in `src/logic/town.ts`, with unit tests.

- **Blocks.** Every block has exactly 100 voters and is described by a whole number `orange` from 0 to 100. Every district has the same number of blocks, so the same number of voters.
- **Uniform swing.** A swing of `s` points (a whole number; negative is toward purple, positive toward orange) moves `s` of every 100 voters in *every block* from one color to the other: a block's orange count becomes `orange + s`, clamped to 0..100. While no block hits the clamp, every district's orange share moves by exactly `s` percentage points, because a district is just its blocks added up. In Swing Town every block is between 10 and 66, so the clamp never applies for swings up to 10 points (a test checks this).
- **Winning.** A district goes to the color with more votes. Exactly half and half is a **tie**, which counts as a seat for neither color. Votes are counted in whole voters, so ties are exact, never a rounding accident. A majority of 6 seats is 4; a tie doesn't count toward it.
- **Seats vs swing.** For each whole-number swing from −10 to +10, the seats each color wins (and ties). The app also reports the smallest swing toward purple at which orange loses its majority.
- **Valid maps.** Every block in a district; every district exactly 36 / 6 = 6 blocks; each district one connected piece, where blocks join only along a side (corners don't count).
- **This is a simplification.** Real elections don't shift evenly in every place, turnout changes, and people aren't blocks. The app says so; it shows one idea, not a model of any real election.

The town and the preset maps are in `src/logic/presets.ts`. The numbers were chosen, by checking all 451,206 ways to split a 6 by 6 grid into 6 connected districts of 6, so that a greedy map can win 5 seats, a safe map keeps 3 at any swing up to 10, and the challenge is possible but not easy: 848 of those maps meet it. Tests pin each preset's whole seats-vs-swing curve and include one map that solves the challenge.

## Develop

Needs Node 22+ and pnpm.

```sh
pnpm install
pnpm dev       # local dev server
pnpm test      # unit tests (vitest)
pnpm check     # typecheck + tests
pnpm build     # production build into dist/
pnpm preview   # serve the build
```

`pnpm e2e` builds the app and runs real-browser checks with Playwright (`e2e/run.mjs`): mixed mouse and keyboard painting keeps focus on the edited block, an unfinished map announces no results, and the printout fits one page on A4 and US Letter for several maps at no swing and at 5 points toward purple. It isn't run in CI. It needs a Chromium: set `CHROMIUM_PATH`, or run `pnpm exec playwright-core install chromium` once.

- `src/logic/town.ts`: swing, district results, ties, seats, the seats-vs-swing curve, map checks and their plain-language messages, the challenge check.
- `src/logic/presets.ts`: the town and the three ready-made maps.
- `src/logic/format.ts`: wording for swings, shares and winners.
- `src/chart.ts`: the seats-vs-swing chart (SVG) and table.
- `src/links.ts`: links to the idea, the code, the JRMF puzzle and Somebody Build This.
- `src/main.ts`: the page (story steps, drawing, slider, print sheet).

Tests cover the math (swing, clamping, exact ties, seats, the curve, map checks and messages), every preset's behavior and a challenge answer, the chart and wording, and the page itself in jsdom (presets, the slider, the story, drawing by tap and by keyboard, error messages, the challenge message, the print sheet and links).

It is a static site (Vite + vanilla TypeScript) with no environment variables, secrets, backend or runtime dependencies. It's deployed on Vercel's free tier at https://swing-town.vercel.app (`vercel.json` sets security headers).
